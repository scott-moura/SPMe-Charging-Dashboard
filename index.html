Fast-charging simulator

A reduced electrochemical model (SPMe with thermal and aging dynamics) of a 5 Ah NMC811 / graphite cell (LG M50), charged two ways: standard CC-CV, and a reference governor that pushes current right up to the limits on the cell's internal states.

Model and assumptions

How the results are computed

Nothing on this page is precomputed, interpolated or looked up. Every slider movement re-runs both charges from the initial state by time-stepping the model below in your browser (simulate() in spme.js, called from run() in app.js); a typical pair takes a few tens of milliseconds. The trade-off chart re-runs 15 more CC-CV charges, plus a bisection of about 9 charges for the fastest in-limits rate, shortly after you stop dragging. The time step is 2 s for the first 30 min, 4 s to 90 min and 8 s after that; the trade-off sweep uses 4 s throughout.

Parameter values, LFP cell (A123 ANR26650M1, 2.3 Ah)

Where the set comes from. The starting point is the Prada2013 parameter set shipped with PyBaMM [15] (file Prada2013.py, PyBaMM 26.10, read on 2026-11-11). PyBaMM attributes it to Prada et al. 2013 [16] and states in the file which values it had to borrow from elsewhere. That set is isothermal and has no thermal or aging parameters, so those are taken from the companion paper on the same cell, Prada et al. 2012 [17], or carried over from the NMC cell.

What was and was not checked. The full text of [16] is behind a paywall and was not read, so the values tagged P below are PyBaMM's transcription and were not compared with the paper. The text of [17] was read on the publisher's web page (Tables III and V); the numbers were extracted from that page text, not from the PDF, so the table columns deserve a second look.   • Capacity window (D). PyBaMM's file gives the 100 % stoichiometries (0.81 and 0.0038) and says they were adjusted for a 2.3 Ah cell between 2.0 V and 3.6 V. Here the anode's 0 % stoichiometry (0.0188) is set so that the window holds the nominal 2.3 Ah, and the cathode's (0.7025) follows from the lithium balance. The open-circuit voltage is then 2.05 V at 0 % and 3.60 V at 100 %.
  • The LFP open-circuit fit. PyBaMM's file codes the first exponential as e^(−150θ). In the arXiv text of [18], as read for this note, the discharge fit has e^(−200θ), with the other three terms identical. This tool follows PyBaMM so that the two can be compared directly. The term only matters below θ ≈ 0.03, that is, in the last few percent of charge, where it changes the cathode potential by up to about 50 mV.
  • LFP is a phase-separating material. This model treats it like any other electrode: Fickian diffusion in a sphere and a single-valued open-circuit curve. It has no two-phase front and no charge / discharge hysteresis (about 20 mV for this material according to P12). That is the same simplification PyBaMM's set makes.
  • The anode limits this cell in the model, probably too strongly. With D_s = 3×10⁻¹⁵ m²/s in 5 µm graphite particles, the anode surface fills long before the bulk. At 25 °C the model predicts plating for CC-CV above about 1.5C, while the manufacturer's data sheet lists a 10 A (4.3C) fast charge to 3.6 V (data-sheet figures as quoted in a third-party listing [20]; the data sheet itself was not readable). Since the plating constants are assumed as well, treat the LFP plating results as a comparison between controllers, not as a statement about what this cell tolerates.
  • Aging model. Prada et al. 2013 [16] has its own SEI model (solvent diffusion through the film, with porosity loss). It is not implemented here; the LFP cell uses the same reaction-limited SEI law and constants as the NMC cell.
  • Reference temperature. PyBaMM's set uses 298 K; this tool uses 298.15 K for both cells.

Departures from the published parameter set (NMC cell)

  • Cell capacity and cathode window. The stoichiometry limits tabulated in C give different capacities for the two electrodes (5.09 Ah for the anode window, 5.61 Ah for the cathode window). Here the anode window defines capacity (5.09 Ah) and the cathode's 0 % stoichiometry is recomputed from the lithium balance (0.849 rather than 0.9084). This puts the open-circuit voltage at 2.55 V at 0 % and 4.19 V at 100 %.
  • Cathode stoichiometry at 100 % SOC (0.2661). Quoted from C from memory and not re-checked against the paper in this pass. The PyBaMM file's initial concentration corresponds to 0.2700, which would lower the 100 % open-circuit voltage by 12 mV.
  • Aging is not fed back. Lithium consumed by SEI growth and plating is tallied but not removed from the anode, and no film resistance is added. Per charge the loss is about 10⁻⁴ of capacity, so the effect on the trajectories is negligible, but this is why the model cannot be run over many cycles as is.
  • Capacity-loss numbers are comparative. The SEI constants are generic and the plating constants are assumed, so read the losses as a comparison between the two controllers, not as a lifetime prediction for this cell.
  • The governor has perfect state knowledge. A real implementation needs a state observer (for example [1]) and margins for model error.
  • Validity. If the electrolyte fully depletes at the anode (very high CC rates, or cold), the uniform-reaction assumption behind the SPMe fails. The dashboard flags this; plating figures in that regime are indicative only.

Numerical method and lithium conservation

Both diffusion equations are discretised with conservative finite volumes and stepped with implicit Euler (one tridiagonal solve each per step). Because fluxes between neighbouring volumes cancel exactly, the scheme should not create or destroy lithium. This was tested with the side reactions switched off, over constant-current charges from 1C to 4C, a 2C discharge, random ±3C pulses with random step sizes, a sinusoid, rest, and closed-loop CC-CV and governor runs from −10 °C to 25 °C, including cases that fully deplete the electrolyte:

  • Solid-phase lithium, ε_s,n·L_n·A·c̄_s,n + ε_s,p·L_p·A·c̄_s,p (0.2827 mol): largest relative drift 2×10⁻¹⁴.
  • Electrolyte lithium, A·Σ ε_e·Δx·c_e (5.368×10⁻³ mol): largest relative drift 2×10⁻¹³.
  • Lithium gained by the anode against charge passed, ∫I dt / F: largest relative error 2×10⁻¹⁴.

The figures above are for the NMC cell. The same cases pass for the LFP cell (solid phase 0.0883 mol, electrolyte 1.2435×10⁻² mol): largest relative drift 5×10⁻¹⁴, 7×10⁻¹³ and 2×10⁻¹⁴ respectively.

These are floating-point round-off. The electrolyte concentration is kept above 1 mol/m³ by a limiter that moves lithium between neighbouring cells rather than adding it, so conservation also holds when the electrolyte depletes. The same three checks run on every simulation in this page and are reported at the bottom of the comparison table. To repeat the full test: node tests/conservation.js.

Cross-check against PyBaMM, NMC cell

The model was compared with PyBaMM 26.10 [15] using its Chen2020 parameter set, against both PyBaMM's own SPMe (the same model class [2]) and its full DFN model [3]. For a like-for-like comparison the extra temperature dependences this tool adds and both side reactions were switched off, and PyBaMM's lumped thermal parameters were used (42.8 J/K, 10 W/m²K). The cases were constant-current charges from 0.5C to 2C at 10, 25 and 40 °C, isothermal and with the thermal model, plus replays of the current profile each controller produces. Each charge runs from 10 % to 80 % SOC.

  • Heat source. Temperature agrees only when PyBaMM's "heat of mixing" option is switched on. This tool's I·(V − U_ocv) includes the heat released as concentration gradients inside the particles relax; PyBaMM's default omits it and ends 3–15 K cooler on these cases. The term is large here because solid diffusion in this cell's cathode is slow.
  • 2C, isothermal. The electrolyte at the anode collector depletes after about 2 min in both SPMe models but not in the DFN (which holds about 130 mol/m³). Voltage is then far off in every reduced model: PyBaMM's SPMe is 330 mV RMS from its DFN and this tool is 140 mV. This is the validity limit already flagged above.
  • Plating overpotential is the weak point. A single representative particle cannot capture that particles next to the separator fill first. Compared with the DFN, the estimate used here reads up to about 13 mV too high through the middle of the charge, and too low near the end.

  • First-minute transient. When current is first applied, the concentration at the anode particle surface rises in a boundary layer thinner than one radial shell (diffusion is slow in this anode). With 20 uniform shells this tool reads 26 mV high 2 s into a 1C charge, and PyBaMM with its default 20 points reads 17 mV low; with 80 shells in both, the two agree within 2 mV. The difference decays to under 1 mV within 2 min. It does not affect the plating minimum, which occurs late in the charge.
  • Plating overpotential. Unlike the NMC cell, the single-particle estimate tracks the DFN closely here (within 0.5 mV at the minimum up to 2C). The electrodes are thin and the electrolyte hardly depletes, so the reaction is close to uniform, which is the assumption the SPMe rests on. In these isothermal cases the 13 mV allowance discussed for the NMC cell is not needed. At 4C the anode surface saturates within about a minute and the models differ in how far below zero the overpotential goes, but agree on when (8.2 against 8.1 min below zero).
